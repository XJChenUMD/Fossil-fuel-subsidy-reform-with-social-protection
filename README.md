# Reallocating fossil-fuel subsidies via social assistance reduces poverty and preserves emissions cuts

## About
This study evaluates whether reallocating explicit fossil fuel subsidies through countries' existing social assistance systems can reduce poverty while preserving the emissions cuts from subsidy reform. We develop a high-resolution global Social Accounting Matrix (SAM) covering 124 countries, 65 sectors, and 201 household expenditure bins, integrating GTAP v11, the World Bank Global Consumption Database, the Luxembourg Income Study, ASPIRE, and the IMF fossil fuel subsidy database. Absent compensation, removing explicit subsidies raises the global poverty headcount while cutting CO₂ emissions; redirecting fiscal savings through existing social assistance reverses the poverty increase, and partial (e.g., 80%) recycling can reduce poverty while preserving emissions cuts and fiscal space.

## Data availability
Restricted-access input data are not redistributed here: GTAP v11 (license required), the restricted-access World Bank Global Consumption Database, and LIS microdata (accessed via LISSY). Publicly available inputs include the IMF fossil fuel subsidy database, IEA fossil fuel subsidies database, ASPIRE, Eurostat HBS, and World Bank Open Data.
